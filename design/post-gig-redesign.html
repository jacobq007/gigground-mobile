The skill picker shouldn't show every skill.

Right now "Which skill?" dumps ~20 bubbles on the page, sits next to a separate three-option "Job type", and won't survive the next category you add. Three ways to make one compact control that scales to any number of jobs — and folds "job type" into it.

Why the current picker breaks

✕ It doesn't scale

Every new job type adds another bubble to a flat list. Twenty today, fifty tomorrow — the page just keeps growing.

✕ It costs a screen of space

Showing all options at once pushes pay and the post button far below the fold, on the one screen you want to feel fast.

✕ It asks twice

"Which skill?" and "Job type" (Errand / Delivery / Other) are the same question split in two — and "Other" is where most real gigs fall.

Idea 2

One field collapses all twenty bubbles. Typing filters everything; five curated chips cover the common cases. Each result carries its own "verified pros" vs "anyone nearby" tag, so job-type disappears.

Best for scale. Add a thousand skills and the UI never grows.

Idea 3
Category first, then skill

Six calm tiles instead of twenty bubbles. Tap one and only its skills unfold — so you see five options, never fifty. Skilled categories reveal a subject row; general ones (Everyday help) skip straight to budget.

Best for browsing. Organised and discoverable when the poster isn't sure what to call it.

Recommendation

Ship Idea 1 (infer from the title) with Idea 2's search as the “Change” fallback. The poster already typed what they need — reading it and proposing a category is the fastest path, and when we guess wrong the search field handles the long tail without ever showing a wall of bubbles. Idea 3's tiles are the right move only if you'd rather people browse categories than type.

  • →Merge skill + job-type into one "Category" control — kill the separate Errand / Delivery / Other row.
  • →Derive skilled vs general from the chosen category, not a manual toggle. Show it as a one-line note.
  • →Never render the full list — infer, search, or drill down; show at most one category's skills.
  • →Add a progress bar so the shorter form feels like a quick 3-step, not an endless scroll.

How it maps to your data

Your SKILL_GROUPS already hold the category → skills tree and a skilled flag per group. All three ideas read straight from it — no new schema.

Inference (Idea 1) is a keyword match from the title to a group; "moving/shift" → General · Moving, "tuition/maths" → Tutoring · Maths. Cheap to ship, easy to improve later.

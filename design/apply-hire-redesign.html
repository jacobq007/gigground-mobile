Design study · apply & hire flow

Fewer bubbles. More working professionalism.

A rewrite of GigGround's apply and hire screens, grounded in how Wellfound, TaskRabbit and Upwork present the same decisions — moving from rings-and-chips clutter to typographic hierarchy, quiet metadata, and one restrained accent. Two directions to choose from.

What we're changing, and why

Grounded in comparable marketplaces

01 Rank in a plain list, best match first

The strongest apps don't decorate the decision — they order it. Candidates arrive ranked, the top one clearly marked, the rest scannable in one column. The facts (experience, rate, distance) sit as text you read, not badges you decode.

Pattern: Wellfound ranked candidate list · wellfound.com/recruit

02 One accent, one money colour — retire the rest

Today a card can carry a coloured ring, three tinted reason chips, an amber strike pill and a status tag at once. We keep a single working-blue for the decision and one calm green for pay; everything else becomes ink and hairlines. Colour now means something.

Principle: clarity over "creative visuals that don't serve function" · rigbyjs.com

03 Progressive disclosure on apply

A worker deciding to apply needs the pay, the fit, and the ask — not a wall of pills. We lead with the figure and a single "why you fit" line, keep details in a quiet key/value block, and put one confident button at the bottom.

Principle: minimise steps, disclose progressively · purrweb.com

04 Match as a figure, not a ring

The circular match ring is the most "app-toy" element on the screen. A right-aligned number with a thin underbar (Direction A) or a labelled meter (Direction B) reads as precise and professional — and takes a quarter of the visual weight.

Craft: encode state in form, keep the accent separate from semantics · design study

Recommendation

And how it maps onto your build

Ship Direction A (“Column”) for the hirer's applicants review — the ranked list is what professional hiring tools converge on, and it removes the most clutter. Borrow Direction B's labelled meter for the worker's matched-offer screen, where a single card wants a little more structure. Both drop the ring, the multi-colour chips, and the boxed stat strips.

  • →Retire the compat ring for a mono figure + underbar / meter.
  • →Collapse reason chips into one written "why" line or a key/value row.
  • →Status & verification become a small check + text, not filled pills.
  • →Actions: two quiet text links + one solid primary, never three equal buttons.
  • →Colour budget: one accent for the decision, one green for money — full stop.
